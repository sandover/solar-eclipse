Reference · measured from the palette itself

A palette holds together when only one thing varies.

Solarized lets hue vary across its full range and holds everything else nearly still. Its eight accent colours sit inside an 11-point brightness band; eight hues taken straight off the colour wheel spread across 65 points. That single difference is most of why one reads as a considered palette and the other as a jumble.

01

Brightness is the unifier

Brightness is what your eye uses to work out structure: what is foreground, what matters, what to look at first. If eight colours differ wildly in brightness, they read as eight different levels of importance, and the page feels noisy no matter how carefully the hues were chosen. Solarized removes brightness from the equation entirely, so the only thing a colour tells you is which kind of thing this is, never how loud it is.

02

The band is bounded by the text itself

The 11-point band is not floating anywhere convenient. Its floor is the comment colour and its ceiling is the body text colour. No coloured token is ever brighter than the prose around it, or dimmer than a comment. Syntax highlighting can only ever sit inside the range plain text already occupies.

03

Everything shares one atmosphere

The background is not black and the text is not grey. Every one of the eight monotones is tinted, and all the dark-mode ones fall within 31 degrees of the same hue. That shared cast is what makes a coloured background look like light in a room rather than a filter laid over neutral text. The tint is strongest in the backgrounds and fades as the tones get lighter, so the text stays legible while the ground carries the mood.

Four pairs, each about five points apart, separated by wide empty bands. In dark mode you read the first pair as backgrounds and the third as text; in light mode it inverts.

04

Every accent is the same distance out

Brightness and saturation are traded against each other so that each accent sits the same perceptual distance from the background. Solarized's red is the most saturated colour in the palette, yet it has less contrast than the body text you read. Vivid and recessive at once. Nothing reaches further off the page than anything else, so nothing grabs you first.

05

Hue does all the work of telling things apart

With brightness and presence pinned, hue is the only free variable, and it is spent deliberately. The eight accents are unevenly spaced, and the background sits in the widest gap in the ring, 36 degrees clear of its nearest accent, so it never competes with a text colour. How far a colour sits from the background tracks how often you see it: function names and strings, which cover most of a screen, sit closest and stay quiet; errors and preprocessor marks sit furthest and are allowed to shout.

06

Nothing touches the edge

A colour pushed to the limit of what a screen can display is what reads as acid. Solarized takes on average 87 percent of the colour available at each hue and brightness, leaving headroom everywhere. The only two it pushes to the limit are its olive and its mustard, which are the two colours people most often say they dislike about it.

07

And some of it is simply taste

Worth being honest about the limits of all this. The documentation says the hues were "adjusted within a small range to achieve the most pleasing combination of colours" after the geometry was set, and that the result was "tested extensively in real world use on colour calibrated displays." So the rules above describe the structure, not every decision. The places where the measurements refuse to fit a clean line are where a person was making judgements, and no amount of arithmetic recovers those.

08

What I had wrong, and when

Each of these should have come out of the first reading rather than out of a complaint.

Missed until you said it looked garish

The accents are placed relative to the background

Rotating the ground while leaving the text colours where they were put the background on top of an accent. In one scheme the two were three degrees apart.

Missed until you said the greens were loud

Nothing is allowed near the edge of the display gamut

I was pinning colours at 100 percent of available saturation in exactly the hues you flagged.

Missed until you said nothing in Solarized feels loud

Presence is held constant by trading brightness against saturation

I had been holding saturation constant instead, which is a different and worse thing to fix.

Missed until you told me to stop and study it

No accent may be brighter than the body text

Two or three accents per scheme were sitting above it. This one is the simplest rule of the lot and it was visible in the first table I ever printed.
